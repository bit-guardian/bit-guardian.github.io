About

I'm an M.S. student in Computer Engineering at Arizona State University (GPA 4.0), graduating in December 2026. My work spans three layers of computing: digital hardware, cloud infrastructure, and AI.

On the hardware side, I write RTL, build coverage-driven testbenches, and take blocks through synthesis and place-and-route to clean signoff. Before grad school, I spent over two years at Park+, growing from intern to SDE 2 while running Kubernetes infrastructure in production. My thesis research is in human-centered AI, where I study LLM interpretability and the human workload behind robot learning.

Working across these layers lets me reason about a system from the transistor to the cluster to the model, and I enjoy roles where that breadth is useful.

// silicon: rtl, dv & pd

2025

Sparse GCN Inference Accelerator, RTL to GDS

I architected a three-stage datapath (transformation, aggregation, and argmax) that uses COO sparse accumulation to fetch only the feature rows an edge touches, then took the 16,590-cell block through synthesis, floorplanning, CTS, and place-and-route in ASAP7 7nm.

  • PPA: 5.26 mW and 88.5 ns of latency against a 100 ns budget, using zero-operand early termination and per-block clock gating.
  • Timing: zero setup and hold violations across 2,196 paths, with clean DRC and LVS in Virtuoso.
  • Verification: the same testbench ran at RTL, post-synthesis, and post-layout to separate timing mismatches from functional bugs.
